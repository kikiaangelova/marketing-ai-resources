# Design Thinking: From an Interview to a Test

**Practical classroom activity · 45–60 minutes · Kiki Innovation & Learning Lab**

## The challenge

Your school library wants to help students find useful resources. Someone suggests an app. Is an app really the answer? **Don't start with the solution. Start with the person.**

## Example workflow (fictional, not actual research)

| Step | Example | What to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explore | “Tell me about the last time you looked for a resource for a project.” | “Would you use our brilliant app?” |
| Observe | The fictional participant cannot check availability before visiting | Treating assumptions as interview findings |
| Define | Students need to check availability before making the trip | Naming a feature instead of a problem |
| Ideate | A simple search or a visible list of available books | Choosing the fanciest technology |
| Prototype | A paper sketch showing search and availability | Building an entire app before testing |
| Test | Ask three people to find a book and observe | Leading users through every click |

## Team task

1. Interview at least two real participants, following your school's privacy rules. Record anonymised observations.
2. Write one problem statement supported by what they actually said or did.
3. Sketch a prototype that takes no longer than 15 minutes to build.
4. Ask at least two people to complete a realistic task with the prototype.
5. Record three improvements based on what happened, not what you hoped would happen.

## Deliverable

A one-page evidence sheet: two interviews, problem statement, sketch, test observations, and three proposed changes.

## Assessment (12 points)

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Open, non-leading questions | 3 |
| Problem statement grounded in real observations | 3 |
| Testable prototype | 3 |
| Honest reflection and improvements | 3 |

**Use AI to prepare questions or organise notes, not to invent participant responses.**
