# Verify AI Answers: A Practical Exercise

**Kiki Innovation & Learning Lab · Version 1.0 · 16 November 2026**

An AI answer can sound convincing and still be wrong. Your task is to decide whether the evidence is good enough to use.

## Learning outcomes

Identify checkable claims, find primary sources, separate facts from assumptions and explain what remains uncertain.

## Worked example

An AI assistant writes: **“80% of students worldwide use AI for learning.”**

This is an **unverified example claim**, not an actual statistic endorsed by this guide.

| Question | What to look for | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who was surveyed? | Location, age and sample size | Unknown |
| What counts as learning? | Homework, revision, tutoring or assessment | Unknown |
| When? | Fieldwork dates | Unknown |
| Is the sample representative? | Sampling method and limitations | Unknown |

**Decision:** Do not use the figure until a suitable primary source is identified. A cautious alternative: “Student AI use varies by study, country and definition; more evidence is needed before making a global claim.”

## Your task (45 minutes)

1. **5 min:** Choose an AI answer and highlight three factual claims.
2. **15 min:** Find primary sources for each. Record the publication date, scope and evidence.
3. **10 min:** Explain contradictions or limitations with a partner.
4. **10 min:** Rewrite the answer with suitable citations and uncertainty.
5. **5 min:** Present the most important correction and your reasoning.

| Claim | Primary source and date | What it confirms | Limitation | Keep / revise / remove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example claim above | None yet | Nothing | Global scope unsupported | Remove |
| | | | | |

## Assessment criteria (12 points)

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Three specific, checkable claims | 2 |
| Relevant primary sources | 3 |
| Date, scope and method considered | 3 |
| Uncertainty presented accurately | 2 |
| Your own reasoning and AI's role stated | 2 |

**Final question:** What do we *know*, what do we *assume*, and what should we test next?
