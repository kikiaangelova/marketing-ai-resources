# Lean Startup: A Small Test Beats a Big Promise

**Practical exercise · Kiki Innovation & Learning Lab · Version 1.0**

## The situation

A student team wants to run a photography workshop. Before planning a big event, it needs evidence that enough people will join.

**Hypothesis:** At least 12 of 40 invited students will register by Friday.

**Success metric:** Registrations — not likes, comments or views.

## Worked example (fictional numbers)

| Step | Action or observation |
| --- | --- |
| Build | Publish one simple workshop description and registration form |
| Measure | 40 invitations, 10 registrations |
| Calculate | 10 / 40 = 25% |
| Learn | The threshold of 12 registrations was not reached |
| Next | Ask a few invitees about timing and content, then test one change |

**The decision:** Do not conclude “nobody wants it”. One small experiment does not establish market demand. Describe what you learned and your next hypothesis.

## Your task

Design an experiment for a product or service idea. State your hypothesis, target audience, minimum success threshold and measurement *before* collecting data. Record what actually happened and what you will test next. Never invent survey answers.

## Submission and assessment

Submit a table with your hypothesis, test, threshold, observation, decision and next step.

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Falsifiable hypothesis | 3 |
| Clear measure and threshold | 3 |
| Honest, traceable observations | 3 |
